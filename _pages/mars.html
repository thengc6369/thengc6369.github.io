Regional dust storm formation

Using atmospheric reanalysis data, I investigated the dynamical precursors of Mars' large regional dust storms, particularly those initiated by cross-equatorial "flushing" storms. Our analysis revealed that strong northern midlatitude wave-3 disturbances and amplified thermal tides typically precede these events. We also found differences between storms originating through the Acidalia–Chryse and Utopia–Isidis corridors, with the former often requiring multiple high-amplitude wave events and the latter capable of developing more abruptly.

Related publication (Icarus, 2025)

Aphelion cloud belt variability and tropical wave dynamics

My research investigates the variability of Mars' tropical water-ice clouds and their interactions with atmospheric waves. Using Mars GCM simulations, we demonstrated that radiatively active water-ice clouds fundamentally modify the tropical wave spectrum, favouring particular eastward- and westward-propagating modes. Cloud radiative feedbacks also play an important role in sustaining the large-scale Tropical Cloud Oscillation (TCO), characterized by shifts in cloud abundance between Tharsis and Syrtis Major.

More recently, we developed an observation-based Aphelion Cloud Belt index to quantify these shifts. Our results suggest that the first major transition each Martian year is seasonally controlled, whereas subsequent transitions are more irregular.

Midlatitude baroclinic waves

Baroclinic waves are responsible for much of the extratropical weather activity on Mars. My research has focused on understanding their seasonal evolution, their pronounced hemispheric asymmetry, and their role in triggering dust storms. In particular, we investigated why northern midlatitude waves are substantially more energetic than their southern counterparts, and how their amplification can lead to frontal dust storms and cross-equatorial dust transport.

Related publication (Icarus, 2026)

Polar vortex dynamics

I investigated the dynamics of Mars' northern winter polar vortex using atmospheric reanalysis data and the analytical Kida elliptical vortex model. We demonstrated that the characteristic oscillations of the vortex around a preferred orientation are controlled by the interaction of the Hadley circulation and topographically forced stationary planetary waves. The opposing effects of these forcings constrain vortex deformation and provide a dynamical explanation for the rarity of polar vortex splitting events on Mars.

Related publication (Icarus, 2022)

Influence of topography on climate

Using controlled Mars GCM experiments with modified surface topography, I investigated how large-scale topographic structures influence atmospheric waves and the hemispheric asymmetry of Martian weather systems. We found that the arrangement of northern highlands and lowlands amplifies baroclinic wave activity, whereas the southern Hellas and Argyre impact basins inhibit wave propagation. These mechanisms explain a substantial part of the stronger northern extratropical wave activity, particularly outside the solstitial seasons.

Related publication (Icarus, 2026)

Thermal tide reconstruction

I am investigating the reconstruction of Martian atmospheric thermal tides from surface-pressure observations recorded by Mars landers, including Viking, Curiosity, InSight, and Perseverance. Combining these observations with Mars GCM simulations, our analysis examines how migrating and non-migrating tidal components interfere to produce the observed diurnal and semidiurnal pressure variations, and how their contributions can be distinguished.
